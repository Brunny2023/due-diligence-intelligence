# Methodology — V2.1

1. Define objective, period, decision context, report depth, and evidence scope.
2. Register sources with provenance, origin, tier, family, independence, date, and limitations.
3. Extract and classify claims without converting company claims into facts.
4. Assess source quality, independence, recency, specificity, corroboration, contrary evidence, completeness, and confidence.
5. Apply the reconciliation-aware discrepancy protocol before asserting any contradiction.
6. Normalize material claims into structured findings. Use the same status, confidence, evidence limitation, decision impact, evidence gap, and management question in Claim Verification, the Diligence Decision Map, and the Executive Decision Snapshot.
7. Assess each diligence dimension qualitatively as Strong, Adequate, Mixed, Weak, Insufficient Evidence, or Not Assessed. State confidence, evidence quality, and the evidence basis. Do not manufacture numerical scores from absent or incomplete evidence.
8. Keep risk severity distinct from evidence confidence and evidence quality. A missing record indicates uncertainty; it does not by itself prove that the related business risk is high.
9. Use objective-specific focus; keep financial analysis evidence-bounded and scenario analysis clearly conditional.
10. Generate the report, apply deterministic quality gates for unsupported certainty and executive-summary consistency, and state what must happen next.

> **Evidence uncertainty must not be converted into artificial risk certainty.** Executive summaries, decision maps, and detailed findings must preserve the same verification status and uncertainty.

A numerical score is permitted only when sufficient evidence, a reproducible method, the scale, inputs, confidence, and limitations are all documented. Otherwise, use the qualitative assessment.

The methodology is an analytical framework, not legal, tax, accounting, regulatory, financial, investment, or other professional advice.
