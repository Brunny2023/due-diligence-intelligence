# Evidence Standards — V2.1

## Evidence hierarchy

| Tier | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | Primary evidence, such as regulatory filings, audited statements, government records, executed contracts, original disclosures, and authoritative registers. |
| Tier 2 | Strong independent secondary evidence, such as reputable financial databases, established news, industry publications, and credible research. |
| Tier 3 | Supporting evidence, such as company websites, marketing, executive interviews, presentations, testimonials, and management summaries. |
| Tier 4 | Weak or unverified signals, such as anonymous posts, forums, unattributed claims, and unverified databases. |

## Analytical attributes

For material evidence, record source quality, source independence, recency, specificity, corroboration, contrary evidence, completeness, and confidence. Confidence is High, Medium, or Low with a short explanation; do not use unsupported numerical precision.

Assess **evidence quality** separately as High, Medium, Low, or Insufficient. Explain the judgment using primary versus secondary status, independence, recency, specificity, corroboration, completeness, and contrary evidence. Evidence quality is not a risk-severity score.

> Repeated copies of the same company claim are one source family, not multiple independent confirmations. A website, pitch deck, executive profile, and marketing post that repeat one internal claim remain company-affiliated unless independent provenance is established.

Preserve whether evidence is user-supplied or independently researched. If external research is unavailable, state that it was not performed. Tier 3 and Tier 4 evidence should not independently verify a material claim.

## Verification-status preservation

Each material claim has a structured status: **Supported**, **Partially Supported**, **Unverified**, **Contradicted**, or **Apparent Discrepancy — Requires Reconciliation**. An executive summary, decision map, risk register, and detailed finding must not say anything stronger than the structured record supports.

When evidence is absent or incomplete, request the specific primary or independent record needed to resolve the uncertainty. Do not convert an evidence gap into a factual adverse conclusion or an unsupported numerical risk score.
