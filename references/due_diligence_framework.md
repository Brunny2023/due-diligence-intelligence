# Objective-Specific Due-Diligence Framework — V2.1

| Objective | Emphasize |
| --- | --- |
| Acquisition Target | Ownership, financial quality, revenue durability, concentration, liabilities, debt, IP, key people, resilience, regulatory exposure, integration, and valuation-relevant findings. |
| Investment Target | Business model, growth quality, unit economics, market, competitive position, capital requirements, financial quality, governance, and downside risks. |
| Vendor/Supplier | Financial resilience, capacity, service continuity, concentration, cybersecurity, insurance, dependencies, and business continuity. |
| Partnership | Strategic fit, capability, reputation, execution capacity, incentives, dependencies, and conflicts. |
| Customer/Client | Financial capacity, payment risk, reputation, concentration, contractual issues, and regulatory considerations. |
| Competitor | Positioning, products, pricing, market, customers, growth signals, strengths, weaknesses, and strategic opportunities. |
| General Company Investigation | Balanced commercial, financial, operational, governance, technology, regulatory, and reputation coverage. |

Adapt coverage to evidence and decision context; do not imply that all categories were verified merely because they appear in the framework. For every material objective-specific finding, preserve its structured verification status in the detailed analysis, Diligence Decision Map, and Executive Decision Snapshot.

Missing evidence is an evidence-quality limitation, not automatic proof of adverse risk. Use **Insufficient Evidence** or **Not Assessed** when supplied material cannot support an assessment, state the needed record, and keep severity, evidence confidence, and evidence quality distinct.
