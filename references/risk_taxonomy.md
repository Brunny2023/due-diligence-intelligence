# Risk Taxonomy and Evidence-Calibrated Assessment — V2.1

## Risk register fields

Every risk record must contain a category, finding, evidence basis, severity, severity rationale, confidence, confidence rationale, potential impact, evidence gap, management question, and decision impact. **Severity is a prioritization aid, not a legal, financial, or investment conclusion. Confidence measures confidence in the risk observation, not confidence that the outcome will occur.**

| Field | Allowed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Severity | Low, Moderate, High, or Critical; assign only where the available evidence supports a rationale. |
| Confidence | High, Medium, or Low; explain the quality, independence, recency, specificity, corroboration, completeness, and contrary evidence considered. |
| Decision impact | Negligible, Low, Moderate, Material, or Potentially Critical; link to the stated decision objective. |
| Evidence gap | State the missing or incomplete record without automatically characterizing the underlying business condition as high risk. |

> **Evidence uncertainty must not be converted into artificial risk certainty.** A missing regulatory authorization record may make regulatory exposure **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**; it does not, by itself, establish a High regulatory risk.

## Taxonomy

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Corporate | Unclear ownership, opaque structure, unexplained subsidiaries, beneficial-ownership concerns |
| Financial | Declining revenue, unexplained margins, cash-flow weakness, debt, concentration, unusual accounting indicators |
| Commercial | Weak customer evidence, single-customer dependency, poor recurring revenue evidence, unrealistic growth or market claims |
| Operational | Key-person or supplier dependency, infrastructure shortfalls, bottlenecks, weak continuity evidence |
| Technology | Obsolescence, security concerns, technical debt, third-party platform dependence, unclear IP ownership |
| Regulatory | Missing authorization evidence, jurisdictional exposure, certification uncertainty, compliance gaps |
| Reputation | Serious complaints, adverse reporting, unresolved controversy, inconsistent public information |
| Strategic | Weak differentiation, intense competition, contraction, fragile business model, execution risk |

## Evidence-calibrated dimension assessment

Each dimension uses a qualitative assessment: **Strong**, **Adequate**, **Mixed**, **Weak**, **Insufficient Evidence**, or **Not Assessed**. Include a concise evidence basis and a separate evidence-quality judgment: **High**, **Medium**, **Low**, or **Insufficient**.

| Dimension | Assessment focus |
| --- | --- |
| Corporate transparency | Verifiability of entity, ownership, and governance |
| Financial quality | Sufficiency and quality of financial evidence, balance-sheet and earnings signals |
| Commercial strength | Customer, revenue, contract, and demand evidence |
| Operational resilience | Delivery capability, continuity, dependencies, and process evidence |
| Market position | Evidence of differentiation and defensible demand |
| Management/key-person risk | Leadership evidence and concentration of capability |
| Technology | Technology viability, security, architecture, and platform dependence evidence |
| Regulatory exposure | Evidence of required authorization and compliance processes |
| Reputation | Quality of reputation evidence and adverse-signal assessment |
| Evidence quality | Completeness, independence, recency, specificity, corroboration, and consistency of materials |

Do not assign an assessment merely because a category appears in the framework. Where material records are absent, state **Insufficient Evidence** or **Not Assessed** and specify the requested record.

## Optional numerical analysis

A numerical score is permitted only when the report supplies the score, scale, inputs, methodology, confidence, and limitations, and the calculation is reproducible from sufficient evidence. The report must not manufacture a numerical score merely because evidence is incomplete. If those conditions are absent, use the qualitative assessment above.

The score, if any, represents only the documented method; it must not be relabeled as a prediction, investment recommendation, or certainty about a business outcome.
